## Quarter 2 Project Brainstorm
 
**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**
 
The most interesting topic was how to write good questions for a forecasting agent. A question has to be clear enough that the agent and a human would read it the same way, and specific enough that it resolves to a definite answer by a set date. Small wording choices can change how well an agent performs, which made me realize question design is as important as the agent itself.
 
**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**
 
I would like to investigate how different agent harnesses perform in our forecasting environment. A harness is the setup around the model: the tools it can call, how it searches for information, and how it structures its reasoning before giving a forecast. I would run several harnesses on the same set of questions and compare their accuracy, to see how much performance comes from the harness rather than the underlying model.
 
**What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**
 
I would change how I create split plans. I have based my splits mostly on overviews and summaries of the dataset. Next time, I would explore the dataset itself first, looking at the actual questions, their categories, and how they are distributed, so that my splits reflect the real structure of the data instead of my assumptions about it.
 
**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**
 
I am interested in ensembling and using an LLM as a judge. With ensembling, I would combine the forecasts of several agents or harnesses and test whether the combined forecast is more accurate than any single one. With an LLM as a judge, I would have a model automatically grade the quality of forecasting questions, which connects back to my interest in what makes a good question and would let us check question quality at scale.
